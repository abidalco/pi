## In one minute

**π makes money fastest as the engine of a small Arabic AI studio, and later as software.**

Sell finished campaigns and short series to Gulf brands and agencies this winter. Use those fees to build the image section, then open π to other teams at $99 a month per team in spring 2027.

- **Studio work first.** Gulf brands pay for finished Arabic work that feels right and is legally safe. The tool market is crowded with rich players: Runway is valued at $5.3B and Higgsfield earns $700M a year.
- **Why π wins later.** None of the 22 competitors checked sells a live team brainstorm or a continuity judge, and keeping characters consistent is the biggest pain in AI film.
- **Year one.** About $25K if careful, $96K in the base case, $243K if it goes well. About 60% comes from studio work.
- **Scripts.** Pitch an Arabic micro-drama to Scene and BlingWood now. Write the lines yourselves so the rights stay clean.
- **First move.** One 60–90 second Arabic showcase film made fully inside π by mid-December.

## How to sell it, start to finish

Eight steps, in order. The first seven sell studio work. The eighth turns that work into sales of π itself. Scripts run alongside.

1. **Pick one offer.** One buyer, one package, one price. For example: an Arabic AI ad with a recurring cast, for Gulf consumer brands, $3,000, delivered in 10 days.
   *Why:* a fixed offer is easy to say yes to. "We do AI video" makes the client do the thinking.
2. **Make the proof.** One 60–90 second Arabic film made fully in π, plus a 30-second behind-the-scenes cut that shows brainstorm, script, shots and film.
   *Why:* buyers trust what they can watch. The behind-the-scenes cut shows the result is repeatable.
3. **Build a short list.** Thirty names: creative directors at agencies and marketing leads at brands in Riyadh, Dubai and Beirut. Start with people you already know.
   *Why:* thirty personal messages get further than three hundred cold ones.
4. **Open with something made for them.** Send the showcase plus three frames of their own brand made in π, with one line: "Your Ramadan campaign as a five-episode series."
   *Why:* a sample of their own brand gets a reply. A deck gets filed.
5. **Name the price on the call.** Twenty minutes. Show the film, ask about their next campaign, offer the pilot at the fixed price. Half up front, two rounds of changes, ownership and AI use written into the contract.
   *Why:* a price said on the call closes. "We'll send a proposal" goes cold.
6. **Deliver fast, in the open.** Share a review link at script, frames and final cut. Hand over in 10 days.
   *Why:* speed is what clients buy AI work for, and visible progress keeps revisions short.
7. **Turn one job into three.** Ask for a monthly retainer with the same cast, one referral, and permission to post the case study.
   *Why:* a recurring cast is the natural next sale, and keeping a cast consistent is what π does best.
8. **Let the work sell the tool.** After three to five jobs, give π free for a month to five friendly studios. Charge the ones who use it every week. Teach the method to the people who ask how you made it.
   *Why:* Boords, Frame.io and Secret Level went in this order: their own jobs first, then the tool, then teaching.

**Scripts, in parallel:** write a 50-episode micro-drama bible and the first 10 episodes in π → cut a 60-second teaser → send to Scene and BlingWood → follow up after a week → agree the rights in writing.

**Three rules:** show before you tell · fix the price and the scope · get half up front.

## What π sells

π is the production office for AI films: one place where a team goes from a messy idea to approved shots, and every image or clip knows its scene, shot and character. The video models (Veo, Kling, Seedance, Runway) are the cameras. π is everything around the camera.

- **A team tool.** Live brainstorm rounds with a bot, votes to promote, drafts and versions of the script. Most AI video tools are built for one person making one clip.
- **Continuity built in.** The Story Bible, the facts ledger and the Judge keep a character, a place and a rule the same from shot 1 to episode 10. It is the pain Hadi fixes by hand every day.
- **One joined chain.** Project → Scene → Shot runs through every section, so a script change shows which images are now out of date.

Built today: Brainstorm and Script, end to end in one app. Specced with a clickable mockup: References. Not built yet: images, video and audio.

## Who would pay

The group with the most money, Gulf brands, buys finished films. The groups that buy software have less money and more choice.

| Who | Their pain | What they buy | Order |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gulf brands, media groups, government bodies | Arabic content at volume, culturally right and legally safe | Finished campaigns and short series | 1st, through studio work |
| Agencies and production houses in Lebanon and the Gulf | Work spread over five tools; characters drift between shots; client notes get lost | A team tool, or a private install on their own models | 2nd, once π has made real work |
| Small AI studios and freelance teams | The same pain, smaller budgets | A monthly subscription | 3rd, crowded and price-sensitive |
| Film schools, workshops, bootcamps | A way to teach AI filmmaking as a team | Class licences and a course | Side income |
| Creators making AI series | Characters must stay the same across episodes | A cheap personal plan | Later |

## Ways to make money, ranked

Sell finished work made with π first, and π itself second. Studio work pays this winter. A subscription pays slowly, and only once the image section exists.

1. **Studio work with π inside.** Arabic AI campaigns and short series for brands and agencies, made faster and more consistently in π. *First money:* weeks. *Verdict:* start now; it funds everything else and proves the tool on paid work.
2. **Team subscription.** A flat monthly price per team, as [Boords](https://boords.com/pricing) and [Frame.io](https://frame.io/pricing) charge. *First money:* about 3 months, after the image section. *Verdict:* the long-term business; charge per team, not per person.
3. **Generation credits.** Video and image runs passed through at cost plus a margin. *First money:* with the subscription. *Verdict:* small and steady; the generators set the price, so it is never the main product.
4. **Private install for production houses.** A setup fee plus monthly support, on their own models and keys. [Utopai](https://forbes.co/2026/05/21/editors-picks/la-joven-de-25-anos-que-aspira-a-convertirse-en-la-primera-magnate-del-cine-de-hollywood-impulsado-por-ia/) made $11M a year in licences within 60 days. *First money:* after 2 or 3 studio jobs to show. *Verdict:* the biggest cheques per client, and the slowest to close.
5. **Teaching the method.** An Arabic course and agency workshops on making AI films as a team. [Secret Level](https://www.thewrap.com/?p=7943003) charges $849–1,299. *First money:* any time after the showcase film. *Verdict:* good side income, and it builds the audience that later buys π.
6. **Grants and prizes.** [AFAC](https://arabculturefund.org/Programs/12) gives up to $20K to produce a short; the UAE's [$1M AI Film Award](https://mena.entrepreneur.com/growth-strategies/dubai-launches-us1-million-ai-film-prize-to-boost-global/496455); [Hub71](https://hub71.com/i-am-a-startup) in Abu Dhabi. *First money:* AFAC's next cycle is in 2027. *Verdict:* free money and publicity for the showcase film; not a business.

## Selling the scripts written in π

The fastest buyer for a great script right now is Arabic micro-drama: series of one-minute vertical episodes. π already makes what that pitch needs: a series bible, the episodes, a shot breakdown and, soon, a short AI teaser.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Arabic micro-drama** | [Scene](https://campaignme.com/scene-is-the-latest-platform-to-explore-arab-storytelling-in-the-region/) (born in Lebanon, [co-producing with Rotana](https://www.arabaddigital.com/en/article/5789-rotana-media-and-scene-join-forces-to-accelerate-the-rise-of-micro-drama-in-the-arab-world)); [BlingWood](https://tbreak.com/blingwood-micro-drama-platform-uae/), Dubai | A 50-episode bible, the first 10 one-minute episodes, a 60-second teaser | About [$10K for a finished series script](https://www.contentasia.tv/features/long-scroll-nuts-bolts-s-serialised-vertical-video) on global platforms; Arab rates are not public | Now. Gulf micro-drama revenue: [$39M (2025) → $74M (2026)](https://www.gulf-times.com/article/730281/qatar/microdrama-users-in-qatar-to-double-by-2030-says-media-city-report) |
| **Brand and creator scripts** | Brands, agencies, YouTube channels | Short scripts, best sold with the studio work | [$150–800 an ad script, $75–300 a YouTube script](https://www.biztoolkit.co/post/freelance-scriptwriter-rates-in-2026-youtube-ads-podcasts) (blog estimates) | Now, small but steady |
| **Arab series labs and grants** | [Mosalsalat Lab](https://www.broadcastprome.com/news/royal-film-commission-jordan-concludes-sixth-mosalsalat-screenwriters-lab/), [Doha Film Institute](https://www.dohafilm.com/funding-industry/funding/grants) TV grant, [AFAC](https://arabculturefund.org/Programs/12) | A long-form series pitch; most need a producer attached | Mentors and buyers; AFAC gives up to $10K to develop a feature | Mosalsalat opens January 2027 |
| **Global spec market** | US producers via the [Black List](https://blcklst.com/register/writers) ($30/mo + $100 a read), [Austin](https://austinfilmfestival.com/about-aff-script-competitions/) ($5K prizes), [Nicholl](https://wgfoundation.org/nicholl) | An English feature or pilot | [WGA minimum](https://www.wga.org/uploadedfiles/contracts/2026_schedule_of_minimums.pdf) for an original screenplay: $61,990–126,899; sales are rare | Slow; keep one script in play |

**Send a script people can see.** Netflix backs a [proof-of-concept program](http://about.netflix.com/en/news/inaugural-proof-of-concept-short-films-screened-in-los-angeles-showcase) that pays $50K per short made to sell a series, and the AI feature [Critterz](https://theoutpost.ai/news-story/critterz-brings-ai-assisted-animation-to-cannes-as-hollywood-debates-ai-s-role-in-filmmaking-25933/) grew out of a short. No writer has yet been reported selling a script on an AI teaser alone, so treat it as an edge, not a promise.

**Keep the words your own.** The US Copyright Office protects only what a human decided, and prompts alone don't count ([Copyright Office](https://copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html)). The WGA does not count AI output as literary material ([WGA](https://wgacontract2023.org/the-campaign/summary-of-the-2023-wga-mba)), and buyers need a clean chain of title to get insured. Write the lines yourselves; use the bot for ideas and the Judge for checks. π's History, which logs who wrote each line and when, then becomes proof of human authorship.

## Cases to learn from

The companies that look most like π made money in the same order: client work first, then the tool, then teaching how they work.

- **[Xawiya Studios](https://gulfnews.com/business/corporate-news/dubai-launches-first-genai-studio-revolutionising-content-creation-in-mena-1.500288638), Dubai, since 2025.** Two media and advertising people sell finished AI campaigns in several languages. By June 2026 they had done work for [Samsung, Visa, Rove Hotels and Lenskart](https://campaignme.com/?p=122925), then added paid consent and compliance services.
  *Lesson:* Gulf brands pay for finished, culturally right work and for legal peace of mind. This is the closest money for an Arabic-speaking team.
- **[Secret Level](https://www.thewrap.com/?p=7943003), Los Angeles, since 2023.** The AI studio behind Coca-Cola's AI holiday ads built its own production platform for its jobs and keeps it in-house. In February 2026 it opened an academy at $849–1,299 that teaches its workflow.
  *Lesson:* an in-house tool earns through the client work it speeds up and through teaching the method.
- **[Boords](https://boords.com/), London, since 2015.** An animation studio tired of storyboards in Photoshop and notes lost in email built its own tool. It launched at [$12 a month plus $12 per team member](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/tools/introducing-boords-easy-use-web-based-storyboarding-tool-149248.html), and is still independent eleven years and 2M+ storyboards later.
  *Lesson:* the lasting money is in versions, comments and client approval. A free plan with a watermark brings people in.
- **[Frame.io](https://signalfire.com/blog/adobe-buys-frame-io), New York, since 2015.** A post-production shop built a review tool for its own jobs. It launched from free to $150 a month, with the $50 plan covering five people. Adobe bought it for $1.275B in 2021.
  *Lesson:* build for the pain you hit on real jobs every week, and price per team.
- **[Curious Refuge](https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/02/21/ai-film-school-trains-next-generation-of-hollywood-moviemakers), online, since 2023.** A couple launched an AI film course at about $749, with office hours and a Discord. About 10,000 students later, the AI studio Promise bought it.
  *Lesson:* a course and a community earn before the software does, and the audience itself becomes worth buying.

## Risks

| What could break | Fallback |
| --- | --- |
| A funded tool (Flora, LTX, Runway) adds teams and continuity next quarter | Own the niche they ignore: Arabic stories, Gulf clients, done-for-you work where trust sells |
| Video generation costs eat the margin | Credits passed through at cost + 20%, or clients bring their own model keys |
| Two people, and images and video are not built | Build only the Image section next; let paid client work fund the rest |
| The bot runs on Hadi's own Claude subscription | Fine for the team's own films; paying outside users need an API key, priced into the plan |
| Hadi's day job is in the same field | Read the employment contract for IP and non-compete lines before selling to the same kind of client |
| Who owns AI-made footage, and model terms | Use models whose terms allow commercial use; state ownership in every client contract |
